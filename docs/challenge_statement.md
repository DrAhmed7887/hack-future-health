# Challenge 06: Population and Workforce Health Early Warning (verbatim)
Read from https://hack2skill.com/event/futurehealthchallenge/ on 7 Nov 2026 (Population Health tab).

**Question:** How might we combine population, environmental, service and workplace signals to identify emerging health risks earlier and support timely, targeted preventive action before they become wider health burdens?

**The Challenge:** Early signs of a population or workforce health threat rarely sit in one dataset. Changes may first appear as unusual service use, laboratory patterns, sickness absence, incident reports, heat or air-quality readings, or clusters within a workplace or district. When these signals are reviewed separately or only retrospectively, a meaningful pattern can remain invisible until illness, injury or operational disruption has already grown. This challenge calls for solutions which support near-real-time monitoring, predictive analytics, and automated prevention triggers. This could potentially include (but is not limited to) clinical, administrative, geospatial, environmental and occupational exposure data, but data completeness, especially reliable geolocation, can limit interpretation. The challenge is therefore to generate an explainable, privacy-preserving early warning that is early enough to change action, specific enough to be useful and calibrated well enough to avoid alert fatigue.

**Objective:** Bring different signals together to spot emerging population and workforce health risks early and help decision-makers act on them in a targeted way.

**Your solution should:**
1. Multi-signal surveillance. Combine at least two materially different signal types, such as clinical or service data with environmental, workplace or community data.
2. Explainable anomaly detection. Detect a rising risk, cluster, or deviation from baseline and show which signals, locations and time periods contributed to the alert.
3. Actionable decision support. Recommend a proportionate preventive action, identify the responsible operational or regulatory role, and show the evidence and assumptions behind the recommendation.
4. Privacy-preserving views. As and where needed, use aggregated data, small-cell suppression, role-based access, and clear boundaries against individual employment or disciplinary use.
5. Operational validation. Back-test against historical or simulated scenarios and report lead time, sensitivity, positive predictive value, false alarms, data latency and behavior when inputs are missing.
6. Health and resilience impact. Show how the solution could support earlier intervention, fewer high-severity incidents or occupational illnesses, reduced sickness absence and lost workdays, and better evidence for policy and regulatory action.
